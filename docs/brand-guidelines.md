# Atlas brand and content guidelines

These rules apply to the product site and the product.

## Positioning

Atlas is the acquisition intelligence platform for companies that buy companies.

- Every acquisition creates evidence.
- Atlas turns that evidence into intelligence.
- That intelligence improves the next acquisition.

The core loop is Understand → Research → Diligence → Decide → Execute → Integrate → Measure → Learn → Next acquisition.

Do not lead with a single capability: Atlas is not "an AI document reader", "an AI chatbot", "an AI diligence tool", "an AI data room" or "an AI project manager". Those are capabilities of one acquisition intelligence system made of six parts:

1. Acquisition Playbook
2. Decision Intelligence
3. Acquisition Memory
4. Seller Claim Verification
5. Continuous Review
6. Built for Serial Acquirers

## Typography

- One modern sans-serif family everywhere: **Mona Sans** (variable).
- Do not use serif or italic display type, mixed typefaces for emphasis, or editorial magazine treatments.
- Create emphasis with weight, size, spacing, opacity and one restrained accent color.
- Numbers use tabular figures (`.num` in the app, `.tnum` on the site), not a monospace font.

## Visual language

Modern, elegant and credible: acquisition infrastructure, not a consumer AI app. Motion and graphics are welcome when they explain the product (the live preview, the acquisition loop, the evidence flow, workspace visuals). Keep effects soft: subtle ambient light, restrained gradients on one accent word, hairlines, no neon.

## Terminology

Never use "case" for an acquisition, deal, workflow, customer engagement or work item. This includes "the case", case management, case workspace, case review, case history, and "case study" when it refers to an acquisition.

Use M&A-native terms: acquisition, deal, target, deal team, workstream, diligence item, finding, risk, decision, action, review, approval, milestone, deliverable, deal document, integration, PMI, synergy, value realization, acquisition portfolio, acquisition playbook, acquisition memory, lessons learned. The full glossary is in `src/data/glossary.ts`.

## Claims

Do not publish third-party performance statistics unless the source has been checked directly, and the wording matches the source's population, timeframe and method. Prefer product-specific statements. Savings are shown only through the estimator, using the visitor's own inputs.
